0.46.1 — Paolo's numbers come from the game, not from memory

THE REPORTED BUG

Beat 2 read "Whatever you pick, 82 a day is the floor". A bare numeral, no
currency mark. It was the Cab's fixed cost ($82/day), written into the prose as
a literal — nothing tied it to CATALOG, so it read as a typo and would have
become a lie the first time a trim was rebalanced. Beat 13 had the same figure,
same way.

THE WORSE ONE FOUND NEXT TO IT

Beat 1 said "Seven and a half thousand dollars and no car" while the topbar said
CASH $800. CFG.startCash was dropped to $500 and then raised to $800 on
2026-07-28 and the dialogue was never revisited, so Paolo has been quoting a
starting balance that has not existed for a year of build time. nextTask()'s
'car' advice repeated it word for word, and two catalogue notes were built on it
too ("you cannot put money down on this on day one — $7,350 with the lender's
reserve, and you have $7,500"; "the last car you can finance on day one" — at
$800, day one finances nothing at all).

THE FIX

Beat text is a `const` string, so live numbers are tokens filled at show time:

  rayFill(t)  {NAME} -> playerName()   (as before)
              {CASH} -> money(S.cash)
              {FLOOR} -> money(floorCost())

floorCost() is the daily fixed cost Paolo calls "the floor": the CHEAPEST fixed
cost in the catalogue while the player has no car (beat 2 fires in the garage,
where the honest claim is "the least this can possibly cost you"), and the sum of
what the held cars owe once there are any — which is what beat 13's "it doesn't
switch off with you" is about. It deliberately EXCLUDES rent and finance, because
both beats say "before rent, before finance" and fixedPerCar() would contradict
the sentence it appears in.

nextTask()'s 'car' task now quotes money(S.cash) — right for the opening and
still right when the task resurfaces later. The two catalogue notes drop their
day-one affordability claims and state car facts only: what a balance reaches is
the garage's job, and canAfford()/affordWhy() already say it per row in money
that is current by construction.

TESTS

test/paolo-numbers.test.js (new) asserts RELATIONSHIPS, not strings — no token
survives a fill, {FLOOR} agrees with CATALOG, {CASH} agrees with S.cash, the
floor excludes rent, no beat contains a bare "82", no catalogue note tells the
player what they have. A rebalance cannot fail these; a regression to a
hardcoded figure cannot pass them.

One test trap worth writing down: /\b82 a day\b/ does NOT prove the currency
mark is missing — \b matches between "$" and "8", so it fails on the correct
output too.
