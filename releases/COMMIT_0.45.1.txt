0.45.1 — the affordable option is the loud one

WHAT WAS WRONG

On the opening's real cash ($800 in Austin) buying outright and financing are
both out of reach and renting is the only door. But Rent was the one row in
.card-acts with no treatment at all — a plain transparent outline — sitting
underneath two .pri gradient buttons that were merely dimmed to opacity .38.
Dimmed-but-coloured beats plain, so the card ranked the two things you cannot
do above the one thing you can.

WHAT CHANGED

- `.avail` on any affordable button: accent border, accent text, and a light
  accent wash (rgba(var(--accent-rgb),.09) — a per-theme token, not a literal).
- A held-back pulse, not a blink: `avail-pulse` rings the button three times
  when the garage renders, then stops. An indefinite animation across nine
  cards is a fairground, and it keeps asking for attention it already has.
  Under prefers-reduced-motion the global animation:none kills the pulse, so
  `.avail` gets a static ring there instead of silently losing the emphasis.
- Rent still never borrows `.pri`'s filled gradient. Buying outright is the
  cheapest way to hold a car and should stay the loudest button whenever it is
  reachable — `.avail` means available, not recommended.
- A caption ("Available now — nothing down") under an affordable button, but
  ONLY when it is the sole way into that car. With money, all three rows are
  affordable and three captions per card across nine cards is noise. buyRow()
  takes the list of rows the card actually renders, so an owned card — which
  hides Buy — doesn't decide "not sole" because of a button nobody can see.

TESTS

test/opening-fleet.test.js gains the $800 case (rent .avail, buy not, the sole
caption, the unaffordable row still explaining itself) and the rich case (every
row .avail, no caption anywhere).
