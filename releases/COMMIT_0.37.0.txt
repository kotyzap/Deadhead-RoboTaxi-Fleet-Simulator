0.37.0 — shifts start at 4x, and Paolo has something to say about 1x

Pavel: the game was built as a real-time job simulator and has been drifting
toward something you can actually sit through — the overnight jump on clock
off, the ride queue, the console panels. Starting every shift at the wall
clock was the last piece pulling the other way. 4x is the default now.

WHAT CHANGED

CFG.defaultSpeed (4) replaces the two hardcoded setSpeed(1) calls: clockOn()
and the resume-a-saved-run path. Nothing else about the economy moves —
speed multiplies the clock, never the money, which the speed control's own
help text already promised and still does. Boot still comes up PAUSED; this
is the speed a shift runs at once it starts, not an autoplay.

The topbar segment's markup default moved from 1x to 4x to match (cosmetic —
setSpeed() rewrites aria-pressed on the first call either way, but a stale
literal in the markup is how these drift).

1x IS STILL THERE AND STILL THE HONEST ONE. A 5.5 km trip really does take
eleven real minutes at the wall clock, and that is still the thesis the
shift report's worked-vs-billed line rests on — it is just now something you
CHOOSE rather than something you start in. Choosing it gets Paolo's blessing
instead of silence:

  Paolo · real time. Thirty-one years I drove at that speed and nobody
  ever offered me a 4× button. The waiting is most of the job — and it is
  the part nobody pays for.

A Comms line, not a #ray card, for the reason paoloBoardCall() already
documents: the card dims the screen, spotlights a control and waits to be
dismissed, which is absurd ceremony for having pressed a speed button.
Once per SESSION and deliberately NOT once ever — no PROG flag. This is
flavour, not instruction; contrast PROG.achvTold, which is once-ever because
it tells you where something lives and is dead weight the second time.
Somebody who comes back tomorrow and reaches for real time again should hear
it again.

The hook lives in the two input handlers (segment click, range slider), NOT
in setSpeed() — setSpeed is called programmatically by clockOn, closeDodge
restoring the pre-mini-game speed, bankruptcy, and boot, and Paolo remarking
on a speed the player did not choose would be nonsense. Verified: a
programmatic setSpeed(1) stays quiet, a click on the 1x button does not.

Beat 9 (the tutorial card that spotlights the speed segment) no longer has
to sell the idea of speeding up, since a shift already starts sped up. It
points at the two ends instead — 20x when nothing is moving, 1x "if you ever
want to feel what I felt."

Verified with jsdom: defaultSpeed is 4 and is a real CFG.speeds entry;
clockOn() lands on 4x with the 4x button pressed, 1x unpressed, and the
range slider on 4x's index; Paolo is silent through a programmatic
setSpeed(1); a real click on 1x drops to real time AND logs him exactly
once; re-picking 1x in the same session does not repeat him; the slider path
still drives setSpeed. Existing suite (8 files) and check-parity both pass.
