0.50.0 — the shift report gets admin's hexagraph

WHAT WAS WRONG

The shift report was fifteen numeric rows and nothing else. The single most
important thing it says — you worked 0.0 hours of a 24-hour billed day — sat
in the same 13px grey row as "Platform take rate", and the one figure that
carried any emphasis was the red net. There was no way to see the SHAPE of a
shift: which part failed, or whether tonight beat last night.

Meanwhile admin/Cars already had exactly the right component for it: a
six-axis hexagonal radar, one fixed colour per axis, one shared legend above
the grid. Pavel asked for that graph to come into the game.

WHAT CHANGED

rpHexRadar() is a port of hexRadar() from deploy/public/admin.html, keeping
that page's presentation deliberately: a fixed colour per axis (Net always
green, Safety always violet, etc.), spokes tinted by their own axis colour,
a neutral var(--accent) fill, chart above the stat lines, one shared legend.
Kept as its own copy rather than shared code — admin.html is a separate
standalone document with no module boundary to share across, and
check-parity.js only ever compares deadhead.html to its own deploy mirror.

The axes differ from admin's, on purpose. Admin's six are Pop./Net/Miles/
Rides/Safety/Reach, normalised against the max across MODELS on that page
load — a comparison that only exists because nine cars are on screen at
once. A single shift has no peer set, and Popularity/Reach are cross-player
aggregates that mean nothing for one night. So Net/Miles/Rides/Safety carry
over unchanged and the other two become Utilisation and Margin, which are
what Act 1 is actually about. Per-axis normalisation, and why:

  Util    worked/billed. Self-normalising, and the thesis of the whole act —
          no invented target needed.
  Net     against 3x the daily floor. A loss reads as zero: a loss has no
          degree worth drawing, the axis just collapses.
  Miles   against the player's own best, so it reads as progress not verdict.
  Rides   likewise.
  Safety  /100, the only axis already on a 0-100 scale.
  Margin  net/gross against a 40% target.

The dashed ghost outline is the best PREVIOUS shift IN THIS CITY, by net.
Three traps it had to avoid, all covered by tests: appendHistoryRow() runs
immediately before shiftReport() (see stopFastForward()), so the row being
reported is already HISTORY's last entry and must be dropped or a new
personal best ghosts itself; both shapes must share their Miles/Rides
denominators or each would normalise against itself and every comparison
would read as a tie; and shift 1 has no previous row, so it gets no ghost
and an empty ghost key that .rp-ghost:empty collapses.

TESTS

New test/shift-radar.test.js, wired into npm test — 32 checks. The
normalisation ones matter most: every axis feeds a polygon coordinate, so an
unclamped value draws outside the outer ring and a NaN yields "NaN,NaN" in a
points attribute, which renders as NOTHING rather than as an error. Both
divide-by-zero cases are real shifts (zero gross for Margin, zero billed
hours for Utilisation) and both are asserted, along with clamping at the top
end, a loss flooring at zero, the ghost picking the right row, other cities
not polluting this city's benchmark, and the chart landing above the first
stat row.

Full suite passes clean (351 checks, up from 319).
