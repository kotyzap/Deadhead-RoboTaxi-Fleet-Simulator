0.42.0 — un-Tesla'd: the marque is Axiom, the cars are drawn

The catalogue no longer names, badges or photographs any real manufacturer.
Nine cars from an invented marque, Axiom, with every spec, price and cost
figure exactly as it was. DESIGN.md §9.4 records the reasoning; this note
records the work.

WHY NOW. §9.3 of DESIGN.md has said "never appears in code, assets, copy or
store listing" since before the cars arrived — then 0.11.0 shipped nine real
product names and nine press photographs anyway. The rule was right and the
shortcut was wrong. Two exposures, ranked honestly:

  1. THE PHOTOGRAPHS were the real problem, not the names. A press kit
     licenses editorial use; a game is not editorial use. A trademark
     dispute opens with a letter, an image claim opens with an invoice.
  2. THE NAMES are defensible as nominative use in prose, and much less
     defensible on nine store-facing product cards in a game whose whole
     argument is that the business model does not close.

NAMES. Cab, Saloon, Crossover, Saloon Long, Crossover Long, Crossover Six,
Saloon Sport, Crossover Sport, Truck — category nouns, no marque can own
them. The marque itself is one string, `const MAKE`, so the next rename is
one edit rather than forty. Meridian was considered and rejected: it is
already the incumbent rival operator in this game's own fiction.

ART. Five original SVG bodies (`CARART`) cover nine cars, because a trim is a
spec change and not a new shape. Filled bodywork, glass, wheels and a contact
shadow, tinted green when the car is in your fleet. The whole photo pipeline
went with them: no <img>, no /carsimg/, no onerror fallback, no .noimg state,
no first-paint reflow — and deadhead.html opened straight off disk now looks
identical to the deployed build, which was never true before.

SAVES — the part that could have hurt. `c.model` is written into every local
save, every cloud slot and every stats row, so 'cybercab' and friends are out
in the world on other people's machines. They are translated, never retired:

  - MODEL_ALIAS + modelId() resolve old ids inside catalog() and hasModel(),
    so a save that never reaches the migration still works.
  - Resolving INSIDE catalog() is load-bearing. The v8 loan migration calls
    catalog() with whatever id the save holds; had aliasing lived only in a
    v9 step, principal would have computed as 0 and every financed car in a
    v7 save would have arrived owned outright — the financing exploit
    reopened by a rename. There is a test for exactly that.
  - SAVE_V 8 -> 9 rewrites ids on load so the old strings stop propagating.
  - hasModel() normalises BOTH sides, so Purpose Built does not un-earn
    itself for a returning player whose car is still saved as 'cybercab'.
  - The Worker's MODEL_IDS whitelist accepts old AND new ids. Dropping the
    old ones would have silently discarded a player's per-model stats.

WHAT DELIBERATELY DID NOT CHANGE. Real city names, real service-area
geometry, real electricity tariffs, real permit regimes, real charger
locations, and the sourcing comments in deadhead.html that say where each of
those came from. Those are factual statements about public information and
they are the reason the economy is worth arguing with. "Robotaxi" stays too:
the bare word was refused registration as generic.

NEW: test/rebrand.test.js — nine alias resolutions, the spec()-fallback trap,
the v7 financed-car trap, achievement continuity, and a brand grep over both
shipped documents that strips comments and MODEL_ALIAS first (the allowlist
and its reasoning are written into the test, not left implicit).

ALSO: docs, marketing copy and all generated OG/explainer images regenerated.
TeslaDesignSystem.md is superseded by InCarDesignLanguage.md.

Not touched: the economy. city.test.js's Act 1 invariants — finance exactly
four, buy none, rent any — pass unchanged, which is the point.
