0.26.2 — Paolo answers a locked city card

Clicking a padlocked city did nothing at all. The button was `disabled`, so it
swallowed the click, took no keyboard focus, and the only explanation lived in a
title attribute nobody hovers on a trackpad.

- Locked cards are now aria-disabled rather than disabled: still clickable, still
  focusable, and switchCity() was already the authority on whether a city may
  open, so the click is safe. cityUnlocked() decides, never the attribute — a
  stale DOM must not be able to open a city.
- Clicking one anchors a Paolo message bubble under that card, in the same
  message-bubble language as #ray (avatar, name line, flattened corner), clamped
  to the viewport like tipShow because a wrapped topbar can put a card on the
  second line.
- One line per city, in the voice of a man who has been driving thirty years and
  counts money out loud. Each names the city BEFORE it rather than the
  requirement in the abstract, because "one shift in Miami" is something you can
  go and do. Under the flavour, the requirement in plain words, tinted with the
  locked city's own tone.
- A city with no authored line still speaks: the fallback builds a sentence from
  the gate, so adding a scenario cannot leave a card mute.
- The bubble goes away on any other click, on Escape, when the strip rebuilds
  (it would be pointing at a dead element), and after 11 seconds.

9 new checks. Mutation-tested: putting `disabled` back on locked cards fails
seven of them.

Also: DEBUG=1 now prints the exception behind a failing check. A swallowed throw
and a returned false looked identical, which cost real time on two failures that
turned out to be the test's own fixture ordering and switchCity being async.
